## Findings

### F1 — the § 7 ledger showed 8 of 32 findings, and the ones it dropped were exactly the recorded set
- **Evidence:** `scripts/retro-evidence.sh` built its ledger from
  `HEAD = re.compile(r"^#{2,3}\s+(.*)$")` — markdown headings only. The last three retros write
  their recorded findings as `- **R1 — …**` bullets under `## Recorded, not proposed`, so the
  ledger printed 3 of 18 for `20260901T120000Z-ship-0005`, 2 of 12 for `20260902T102422Z-ship-iterate-0005`
  and 3 of 12 for `20260902T130637Z-realign-0005`. `retros/README.md` claims "every one of its
  finding headings" and calls the ledger the second-occurrence mechanism.
- **Judgement:** happened on this run — the first consumer of #17's ledger. F2 below is a
  second occurrence I could only find by full-text grepping five retro bodies, which is the
  cost the ledger exists to remove.
- **Disposition:** proposed. `scripts/retro_findings.py` now owns the extraction; the ledger
  prints from it and `check-docs.py [retro-ledger]` holds every file in `retros/` to it, by
  counting leads against the file's own `findings:` frontmatter. Probed both ways: with the
  bullet form removed the gate fails on all three files, with it restored 20 checks pass.

### F2 — `/realign` had no wording for a run with nothing to bump, nothing to pin and nothing to promote
- **Evidence:** `.claude/commands/realign.md` step 4 said `git switch -c chore/realign-<change>`
  … `git commit` unconditionally, and step 5 said "commit on the same branch" and "push it and
  open the pull request". This lane ran with all three submodules already matching `repos.lock`
  and no change named: it correctly did none of it, and had to invent the exemption. Steps 2
  and 3 had no skip rule either, so an agent reading literally pays a `make up` plus a full
  baseline after every harness merge, and one that skips is inventing.
- **Judgement:** second occurrence. `retros/20260902T130637Z-realign-0005-observability-phoenix.md`
  R6 — "`/realign` still does not say what to do when no change is named" — recorded the
  narrower half and marked it first occurrence. By `docs/adr/0009` a harness pull request merges
  once per lane, so this is the most frequent merge this repo sees.
- **Disposition:** proposed, as prose in the lane. New step 0 names the precondition, says to go
  to step 7 without a branch or a commit, and makes the only honest result of not running steps
  2 and 3 `not verified` with the proving diff quoted — never a pass.

### F3 — exercising `iterate.sh`'s own argument handling dispatched two live sessions and moved two submodules off `main`
- **Evidence:** the friction log, item 5: verifying the new `--only` guard ran
  `./scripts/iterate.sh 0004-rag-knowledge-base --only ,` against the real script; `fanout.sh`
  launched two `claude -p` sessions and switched chatbot-backend and frontend to
  `feat/0004-rag-knowledge-base`. Killed in ~120s, trees clean, everything restored. `fanout.sh`
  has `--dry-run` (`fanout.sh:27,49,194`); `iterate.sh` had none, so the workaround was copying
  the script to a scratchpad, rewriting `ROOT=` and truncating it.
- **Judgement:** the guard bug is fixed in #17; the untestability is not, and it is what made a
  guard probe cost two real dispatches. The blast radius is a product repo's branch state, which
  is the one thing §5 says this harness must never touch by accident.
- **Disposition:** proposed. `iterate.sh --dry-run` validates arguments, prints the resolved
  plan and exits 0 before the run directory or either lock exists. Probed: the three `--only`
  guards now reproduce at exit 2 with no dispatch, `.locks/` and `git status` unchanged.
